人間形成カリキュラム · Human Formation Curriculum

The Inner Life
50 Anime Films · 50 Manga — A Curriculum for Human Development

Five Phases · Ten Themes · One Unfolding Life

This is not a watchlist. It is a curriculum — a sequenced journey through five phases of human growth: from identity and shadow, through grief and love, to freedom, purpose, and finally wisdom. Every film and every manga has been chosen for what it teaches the self about itself.

Begin at Phase One. Resist the urge to skip.

I
Phase One

Self, Shadow & the Masks We Wear

Who are you beneath what you were told to be? These works crack open identity, confront self-deception, and begin the long work of knowing one's own face.

Core question: "What parts of myself am I hiding — from the world, and from myself?" Jung called this the Shadow. These works make it visible.

Reflection Practice

Questions to Carry Through Each Phase

These are not questions to answer once. They are questions to return to — after each work, and again years later.

I

Self & Shadow

What version of yourself do you perform for others — and how does it differ from the one you are alone at 3am?

Which character in these works do you find yourself defending most vigorously? What does that defence say about you?

What thing about yourself do you most hope no one notices? When did you decide to hide it?

If a Satoshi Kon film were made about your inner life — what genre would it be? Horror? Romance? Tragedy?

II

Grief & Impermanence

What loss have you not yet fully felt? Not because it was small — but because feeling it fully felt too dangerous?

Grave of the Fireflies is not only about war. What is the pride in your own life that you have let harm someone who needed you?

The Japanese concept mono no aware — the ache of beauty that will not last — what in your life do you not let yourself love fully because it will end?

After Mushishi: what in your life is resisting natural cycles — and what would it take to let it pass?

III

Love & Relation

Liz and the Blue Bird is about a love that requires one person's freedom. Which relationship in your life is asking you to do the same — to love by releasing?

What do you want from others that you have not yet learned to give yourself?

Spirited Away: love as labour. Think of the last time you chose to act lovingly when you did not feel loving. What happened?

Nana: two women who never say what they need from each other. Who in your life are you doing this with right now?

IV

Purpose & Resistance

Fullmetal Alchemist says: you cannot gain without giving something of equal value. What have you been trying to get without paying the real price?

Attack on Titan refuses to give you a hero. Which character are you most like — not who you admire, but who you actually resemble in your behaviour?

In This Corner of the World: what is the beauty in your ordinary life that you are not seeing because you are too focused on what is wrong with it?

Mind Game: if you were given your life back right now, this moment — what would you do differently before nightfall?

V

Wisdom & Freedom

Aria is set on a future Mars terraformed into a Venice of canals. Its characters are gondoliers. They have chosen smallness. Is there something in your life that you have rejected as too small — that might actually be enough?

Yokohama Kaidashi Kikou ends the world gently. When things fall apart, what remains? What in you survives the falling apart?

Porco Rosso refuses to be human in a world that has disappointed him. Is there a part of yourself that has made the same refusal? And is it serving you?

The Walking Man walks. No destination. No achievement. Just encounter with what is. When was the last time you allowed yourself to exist without purpose — and what did you find?

These books are not supplements. They are the theoretical framework that makes the curriculum cohere as a practice of living.

Systematic dismantling of the conditioned self. Every chapter is a scalpel for the same territory Satoshi Kon enters from the side.

How systems colonise the psyche. Pairs devastatingly with Paprika, Perfect Blue, and Attack on Titan.

Grief's logic, written from inside it. What Takahata films, Didion writes without softening.

Read with Phase II–III

How meaning persists inside the worst conditions. The philosophical spine of In This Corner of the World and Grave of the Fireflies.

Read with Phase III

Love as a learned capacity, not a feeling that arrives. The psychological architecture behind every relationship in this curriculum.

Read with Phase III–IV
Siddhartha

All phases of life as necessary. Vagabond's Musashi is Siddhartha in a different tradition — the path is the same.

The cognitive architecture behind the propaganda that works on the characters in Akira and 20th Century Boys — and on you.

Read with Phase V
The Untethered Soul

The witness self that watches the self. The philosophical companion to Angel's Egg, Aria, and Yokohama Kaidashi Kikou.

Usage Notes

How to Use This Curriculum Without Ruining It

01

Do not binge

One work per week is the maximum. Two is a mistake. These films and manga are not content — they are events. Give each one the days it needs to do its work on you. Binging is the mind's way of staying in control. The curriculum requires you to lose control a little.

02

Keep a notebook, not a rating

Rating a film is the mind asserting authority. Writing is the mind opening. After each work, write for ten minutes without editing. Do not write about the story. Write about what moved in you. The notebook is the real curriculum.

03

When a work bores you, stay

Boredom in front of Angel's Egg or The Walking Man is information. It means something in you is resisting the pace. That resistance is what the work is for. Sit with it. The boredom is a door.

04

When a work breaks you, stop

Grave of the Fireflies, Oyasumi Punpun, and Berserk are not for every season. If one strikes something raw, put it down. Come back in six months. The curriculum has no deadline. You are not behind.

05

The order is a suggestion, not a law

Use the thematic cross-index if life has sent you to a specific theme. The curriculum meets you where you are. Phase V is not a graduation — it is simply a different set of questions. You will cycle back to Phase I. That is the point.

06

The works marked Core are non-negotiable

If you only have time for thirty works in your life, let them be the ones marked Core. Everything else is depth and texture. The Core works are the bones of the curriculum — they will hold even if the rest falls away.

Master Voices

Seven Creators Whose Work Forms a Complete Study

Watching all the work of any one of these artists is itself a curriculum.

Filmmaker · 🎬

Hayao Miyazaki

Studio Ghibli · 1984–2023

The complete filmography is a single sustained argument that the world, despite everything, is worth being alive in. No other filmmaker has made that case with such consistent force.

In this curriculum: Nausicaä · Totoro · Kiki · Porco · Mononoke · Spirited Away · Howl's · Ponyo · Wind Rises · Heron
Filmmaker · 🎬

Satoshi Kon

1997–2010 · Died at 46

Every film is an investigation of how the mind constructs reality and then mistakes the construction for the world. A complete filmography is four films and one series — each essential.

Perfect Blue · Millennium Actress · Tokyo Godfathers · Paprika · (Series: Paranoia Agent)
Filmmaker · 🎬

Isao Takahata

Studio Ghibli · 1968–2013

The most underrated of Ghibli's two masters. Where Miyazaki builds worlds, Takahata witnesses lives. His films destroy and illuminate with equal patience.

Grave of the Fireflies · Only Yesterday · Pom Poko · My Neighbours the Yamadas · Kaguya
Filmmaker · 🎬

Masaaki Yuasa

Science SARU · 2004–present

Pure vitality in animated form. Every film is a different argument for the same thing: to be alive is to move, change, collide with the world, and survive the collision with more life than before.

Mind Game · Tatami Galaxy · Lu Over the Wall · Night is Short · Ride Your Wave · Inu-Oh
Manga Artist · 📖

Naoki Urasawa

1986–present

The greatest living manga artist for readers who care about psychological depth. Every work is a question about what it means to be human in a system that would rather you weren't.

Monster · 20th Century Boys · Pluto · Billy Bat · Asadora!
Manga Artist · 📖

Inio Asano

2000–present

The chronicler of young adulthood's particular failures. No one draws the gap between the life expected and the life being lived with more precision or more mercy.

Oyasumi Punpun · Solanin · Nijigahara · Dead Dead Demon's · What a Wonderful World
Manga Artist · 📖

Jiro Taniguchi

1972–2017 · The European Mangaka

The slowest, quietest master. More widely read in France than in Japan. His work teaches attention — the practice of noticing what is in front of you as it actually is.

The Walking Man · Summit of the Gods · A Distant Neighbourhood · The Quest for the Missing Girl
