The Complete Guide

This encyclopedia covers 200 anime works — 100 films and 100 series — spanning 60 years of the medium, from Tezuka's foundational experiments to the 2024 season. It is not a ranking. It is a map of what anime has achieved, organised so that any viewer — newcomer or veteran — can find the work most likely to reward their time. The ROI rating is the guide's central editorial act. ESSENTIAL entries belong in any serious conversation about animated storytelling in any medium. HIGH entries are exceptional works that deliver on every hour invested. UNIQUE GEM entries are irreplaceable — they exist in no other form and cannot be substituted. HIDDEN entries

are works of genuine merit that most viewers, including dedicated anime fans, have not seen. Three rules the guide follows: (1) For Puella Magi Madoka Magica, Odd Taxi, and Astra Lost in Space — go in blind. Prior knowledge destroys specific effects these series depend on. (2) For Steins;Gate and Monster — commit before judging. Both series require a substantial initial investment before they reveal themselves. (3) For The Promised Neverland and Sword Art Online — specific stopping points are noted. Continuing past them reduces,

rather than increases, what the series gave you. The guide covers every major studio — Ghibli, Madhouse, MAPPA, ufotable, Bones, Production I.G., Kyoto Animation, Wit, Trigger, and dozens more. It covers every genre — mecha, magical girl, isekai, sports, slice of life, psychological horror, historical epic, romance, and avant-garde experiment. It covers films from 46 minutes to 155

minutes and series from 11 episodes to 367. One final note: anime's greatest works are not lesser versions of live-action cinema. The medium's capacity to render the internal as external — to make a character's emotional state the literal weather, the literal colour of the world, the literal physics of their environment — is unique. The works in this guide exploit that capacity. Begin anywhere. You

will not run out. The Definitive Anime Encyclopedia: 100 Movies + 100 Series | Page 2

FANTASY / ADVENTURE

ESSENTIAL

  1. Spirited Away

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli · 125 min · 2001 A sulking 10-year-old enters a spirit world when her parents are turned into pigs, and finds she must work in a bathhouse for gods. Miyazaki's most complete vision — folklore, ecological grief, and the shock of growing up distilled into 125 hand-painted minutes.

WHY IT MATTERS: The only anime to win the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. The highest-grossing Japanese film ever made. Every frame is

intention. No other film rewards a child and an adult so differently — and both leave changed.

TIME INVESTMENT: 125 min. Returns a lifetime of imagery. The single best anime entry point for any human being.

WAR / TRAGEDY

ESSENTIAL

  1. Grave of the Fireflies

Isao Takahata · Studio Ghibli · 89 min · 1988 Japan 1945. Two orphaned siblings — teenage Seita and four-year-old Setsuko — survive the firebombing of Kobe and then slowly don't. Released on a double bill with My Neighbor Totoro. The contrast was intentional and devastating.

WHY IT MATTERS: Roger Ebert called it one of the greatest war films ever made. It is not for children. The most complete anti-war statement in animation

— grief, pride, and innocence destroyed with total honesty. TIME INVESTMENT: 89 min of pure devastation. Returns an understanding of war's civilian texture no live-action film has matched.

SCI-FI / CYBERPUNK / ACTION

ESSENTIAL

  1. Akira

Katsuhiro Otomo · TMS Entertainment · 124 min · 1988 Neo-Tokyo, 2019. A biker's friend develops uncontrollable psychic powers after a military experiment. Based on Otomo's 2,000-page

manga, compressed into 124 minutes of hand-drawn animation still unlike anything else. WHY IT MATTERS: The film that brought anime to the world. Drawn at 24fps with 327 custom colours. It invented the cyberpunk city The Matrix borrowed.

Still the technical peak of hand-drawn animation. TIME INVESTMENT: 124 min. Returns the entire visual language of cyberpunk and the founding document of anime's global reach.

Satoshi Kon · Madhouse · 81 min · 1997 A pop idol retires to become an actress. Her grip on reality dissolves. Darren Aronofsky bought the rights specifically to recreate the

ESSENTIAL

  1. Millennium Actress

Satoshi Kon · Madhouse · 87 min · 2001 Two documentary filmmakers interview a legendary retired actress. Her memories and her films bleed into each other until the line

dissolves. Kon's most emotionally generous film. WHY IT MATTERS: The most beautiful film about cinema and memory. The structural conceit — documentarians inside the memory-films — executed with

such elegance you stop noticing the technique. TIME INVESTMENT: 87 min. Covers a lifetime and makes you feel every year. Among the most emotionally efficient films in any medium.

ESSENTIAL

  1. My Neighbor Totoro

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli · 86 min · 1988 Two girls move to rural Japan while their mother is hospitalised. They discover the forest is inhabited by a large gentle creature. Nothing

bad happens. Everything matters. No antagonist, no plot — only life. WHY IT MATTERS: Distils Ghibli's entire philosophy. Miyazaki filmed a Japan that no longer exists. The catbus is the greatest animated creature ever

designed. The most radical thing about it: pure presence. TIME INVESTMENT: 86 min. The highest joy-per-minute ratio in cinema. Essential for anyone who has ever been a child.

FANTASY / TRAGEDY / DRAMA

ESSENTIAL

  1. The Tale of Princess Kaguya

Isao Takahata · Studio Ghibli · 137 min · 2013 The oldest Japanese narrative — The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter — reimagined. A miniature girl found in bamboo grows up, is taken to the capital as a princess, and the film quietly reveals why she is here and what she must leave behind.

WHY IT MATTERS: Takahata's final and greatest film. Animated in Japanese ink-wash style — lines visible, watercolour bleeding — so the impermanence

of the images matches the impermanence of the story. TIME INVESTMENT: 137 min. One of cinema's great sad films. Returns a rethinking of what animation can look like.

ROI: SCI-FI / PHILOSOPHICAL / ACT..

ESSENTIAL

  1. Ghost in the Shell

Mamoru Oshii · Production I.G. · 83 min · 1995 2029. A cyborg agent hunts a hacker called the Puppet Master who can hijack people's consciousness. The deeper she goes, the more she

questions whether she has a consciousness at all. WHY IT MATTERS: The Wachowskis watched this before The Matrix. James Cameron called it the most realistic animation ever. It asks the central question of AI philosophy — what constitutes consciousness — with more rigour than any live-action film.

TIME INVESTMENT: 83 min. Returns the foundational text of cyberpunk philosophy in cinema. The questions have only become more urgent.

ROMANCE / FANTASY / DRAMA

ESSENTIAL

  1. Your Name (Kimi no Na wa)

Makoto Shinkai · CoMix Wave Films · 106 min · 2016 A girl in rural Japan and a boy in Tokyo switch bodies. Their attempts to communicate become a love story about connection across time

and space. The highest-grossing anime film of its era. WHY IT MATTERS: The structural twist at the midpoint transforms the film completely — it becomes something more painful and more meaningful. Shinkai

makes the most beautiful rain and light in animation. TIME INVESTMENT: 106 min. The film most likely to make you cry on a train. Returns a complete emotional experience.

ROI: DRAMA / PSYCHOLOGICAL / ROMA..

ESSENTIAL

  1. A Silent Voice (Koe no Katachi)

Naoko Yamada · Kyoto Animation · 130 min · 2016 A boy who bullied a deaf girl spends years trying to atone — and to forgive himself. The deaf girl must decide if forgiveness is

something she owes anyone. The most honest anime about guilt, disability, and repair. WHY IT MATTERS: Yamada's formal sophistication — the protagonist literally cannot look at faces, shown through visual obscuring — embeds the

psychological portrait into the film's grammar. Devastating and truthful. TIME INVESTMENT: 130 min. More honest about guilt and repair than almost any live-action film on these subjects.

ROI: HIGH FANTASY / ROMANCE / ADVENTURE

  1. Howl's Moving Castle

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli · 119 min · 2004 A young hat-maker cursed to live as an old woman takes refuge in the walking castle of the vain, secretly terrified wizard Howl. Set during a meaningless war neither can stop. Miyazaki's anti-Iraq War film hidden inside a fairy tale.

WHY IT MATTERS: The castle's design is one of cinema's great mechanical fantasies. Sophie's age-shifting is the most elegant animation of character growth

ever executed. Miyazaki refused to attend the Academy Awards in protest of the Iraq War. TIME INVESTMENT: 119 min of the most inventive production design in animation, and a love story between two broken people.

ROI: HIGH SCI-FI / FANTASY / ADVENTURE

  1. Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind

Hayao Miyazaki · Topcraft · 117 min · 1984 A thousand years after ecological catastrophe, the world is covered by a Toxic Jungle. Nausicaa can breathe inside it and communicate with its giant insects. When war comes, she must understand the jungle's secret before stopping it.

WHY IT MATTERS: The film before Ghibli that made Ghibli inevitable. Nausicaa remains Miyazaki's greatest female protagonist. The ecological philosophy

that defines all his work is here in its purest form. TIME INVESTMENT: 117 min. The founding text of Miyazaki's entire worldview — everything Ghibli built begins here.

purely thrilling adventure. WHY IT MATTERS: Flawless adventure mechanics. Every chase earned, every revelation lands. The image of the abandoned floating city reclaimed by trees

Mamoru Hosoda · Studio Chizu · 117 min · 2012 A woman falls in love with a wolfman. They have two children who can shift between forms. He dies. She raises them alone in rural Japan, not knowing which world they will choose. A film about parenting as preparing children to leave you.

ROI: HIGH BIOGRAPHICAL / DRAMA / ROMANCE

  1. The Wind Rises

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli · 126 min · 2013 A fictionalised biography of Zero fighter designer Jiro Horikoshi — who dreams of beautiful aircraft in a Japan drifting toward

catastrophe. Miyazaki's most personal and most controversial film. WHY IT MATTERS: The only Ghibli film where beauty and tragedy are the same thing — the planes are exquisite; the war they serve is not. A meditation on

the ethics of craft by a man who knows what his own art has enabled. TIME INVESTMENT: 126 min. Returns the most complex moral question Miyazaki ever posed. Demands multiple viewings.

ROI: HIGH ROMANCE / FANTASY / DRAMA

  1. Weathering with You (Tenki no Ko)

Makoto Shinkai · CoMix Wave Films · 112 min · 2019 A runaway boy in Tokyo meets a girl who can stop the rain. The city is drowning. Their love and the weather are the same crisis. The

ending refuses the comfortable choice — the personal over the societal. WHY IT MATTERS: Shinkai's most formally daring film. The rain is the most beautiful in cinema. The ending was criticised and celebrated equally — he

chose individual love over the city's survival. TIME INVESTMENT: 112 min. Returns extraordinary visual beauty and a genuinely debated moral ending.

  1. In This Corner of the World

Sunao Katabuchi · MAPPA · 129 min · 2016 A young woman marries into a family in Kure, near Hiroshima, in WWII Japan. We live her daily life in extraordinary detail —

  1. Mind Game

Masaaki Yuasa · Studio 4C · 103 min · 2004 A young man is shot by a yakuza member and negotiates with God for a second chance. What follows is the most visually anarchic

anime feature ever made — styles shifting every scene, pure creative velocity. WHY IT MATTERS: Established Yuasa as animation's most visually inventive director. Every scene uses a different technique. A declaration of independence

from every convention — and it works emotionally despite total formal chaos. TIME INVESTMENT: 103 min of the most visually free film in anime. Nothing looks like this. Nothing tries to.

  1. Barefoot Gen (Hadashi no Gen)

Mori Masaki · Madhouse · 83 min · 1983 August 6, 1945, Hiroshima — from manga creator Keiji Nakazawa's own survival. A boy witnesses the atomic bomb and its aftermath.

The bomb sequence is among the most harrowing 10 minutes in film history. WHY IT MATTERS: The atomic bomb depicted with documentary honesty in animation — the only medium capable of rendering it without exploiting real

footage. Essential historical document. TIME INVESTMENT: 83 min. Returns a survivor's-eye-view of Hiroshima unique in all animation.

ROI: HIGH COMEDY / DRAMA / ADVENTURE

  1. Tokyo Godfathers

Satoshi Kon · Madhouse · 92 min · 2003 Three homeless people — an alcoholic, a transgender woman, and a runaway girl — find an abandoned baby on Christmas Eve and must

return her to her parents. Kon's warmest, most generous film. WHY IT MATTERS: The city of Tokyo becomes a character of extraordinary vitality. The film trusts in human kindness more than anything else Kon made

— coincidence and compassion instead of psychological dread. TIME INVESTMENT: 92 min. The feel-good Satoshi Kon film — which undersells its depth considerably.

ROI: HIDDEN FANTASY / MUSICAL / ADVENTURE

  1. Lu Over the Wall

Masaaki Yuasa · Science SARU · 112 min · 2017 A withdrawn boy in a fishing village discovers music attracts mermaids — and one, Lu, is irresistibly drawn to humans and dancing.

Yuasa's most joyful film — colour, movement, pure delight. WHY IT MATTERS: Won the Jury Prize at Annecy, the world's most prestigious animation festival. Achieves the kind of pure animated joy only Miyazaki

managed before. Music, water, motion as unified language. TIME INVESTMENT: 112 min of pure animated joy. One of the most underrated films of the decade in any medium.

ROI: HIGH SCI-FI / ADVENTURE / FAMILY

  1. Summer Wars

Mamoru Hosoda · Madhouse · 114 min · 2009 A math genius and a girl accidentally unite to defend a global virtual world from a rogue AI — alongside her enormous extended family

gathering for a grandmother's birthday. Technology and tradition as the same battle. WHY IT MATTERS: Hosoda's most structurally elegant film — the family chaos and the digital crisis mirror each other perfectly. The final act combines

grandmotherly authority and computer code in the most satisfying way in anime. TIME INVESTMENT: 114 min. Returns warmth, spectacle, and the most satisfying family-vs-apocalypse film in animation.

ROI: HIGH FANTASY / ACTION / DRAMA

  1. The Boy and the Beast

Mamoru Hosoda · Studio Chizu · 119 min · 2015 A runaway boy stumbles into a parallel beast world and becomes the apprentice of a rough warrior. The father-son bond — all the things

it can be — is the real subject. Hosoda's most structurally satisfying film. WHY IT MATTERS: The beast world conceived with complete visual originality. Tokyo observed with documentary specificity. The emotional payoff is

total. The best film about mentorship in animation. TIME INVESTMENT: 119 min of father-son cinema disguised as fantasy.

ROI: UNIQUE

ROI: HIDDEN DRAMA / MYSTERY / SLICE OF L..

  1. Colorful

Keiichi Hara · Sunrise · 126 min · 2010 A soul given a second chance inhabits the body of a middle-school boy who committed suicide and must discover the mistake he made

in his previous life while learning why the boy he inhabits gave up. WHY IT MATTERS: The most honest anime about adolescent depression and family dysfunction. The mystery structure serves a psychological portrait of

extraordinary depth. Barely known outside Japan — a genuine lost treasure. TIME INVESTMENT: 126 min. Returns one of the most compassionate and honest portraits of teenage suffering in animation.

ROI: HIGH DRAMA / FANTASY / MYSTERY

  1. When Marnie Was There

Hiromasa Yonebayashi · Studio Ghibli · 103 min · 2014 A withdrawn girl sent to the countryside for her health begins a secret friendship with a mysterious blonde girl at an old marsh house. A

ghost story that turns out to be something more intimate and more devastating. WHY IT MATTERS: Ghibli's most emotionally precise film about loneliness and the terror of being unloved. The friendship between the two girls is animated

with a delicacy that makes the final revelation unbearable in the best way. TIME INVESTMENT: 103 min. Returns the most precise portrait of childhood loneliness in the Ghibli catalogue.

ROI: HIGH FANTASY / ADVENTURE / DRAMA

  1. The Secret World of Arrietty

Hiromasa Yonebayashi · Studio Ghibli · 94 min · 2010 Tiny people called Borrowers live beneath the floorboards of a house, taking small things humans won't miss. A teenage Borrower is

discovered by a sick human boy. An exquisitely detailed portrait of a miniature world. WHY IT MATTERS: The most beautifully detailed Ghibli film — every ordinary object reimagined at the scale of an insect. Based on Mary Norton's beloved

novel with Ghibli's environmental consciousness added to the source. TIME INVESTMENT: 94 min. Returns a complete reimagining of domestic space at a different scale — endlessly inventive.
