UNDERSTANDING BEING

HUMAN

Films & Documentaries That Open a New Domain of Human Experience Like Won't You Be My Neighbour — films that make you see a part of human life you had never truly seen before. Fred Rogers did one thing that almost no one does: he sat with a child, fully present, and said — you are enough, exactly as you are. Watching that documentary, you understood something about human goodness, about childhood, about what television could have been, that you couldn't have gotten from any lecture or book. These films do the same — each one opens a door into a domain of human experience you may never have entered: death, dementia, poverty, forgiveness, addiction, solitude, war, genius, disability, faith, grief, creativity, the body, the mind. You finish them knowing something you didn't know before — not as information, but as felt understanding. Organized by the domain of human life each film opens. —1—

THE FILMS

Each entry shows: the domain of human experience it opens, and specifically what you will understand after watching that you didn't understand before. Both documentaries and dramas are included — sometimes fiction reaches truth that documentary cannot. ■ Childhood & How Children Actually Experience the World Fred Rogers spent his life insisting that childhood is serious. These films agree. 1. Won't You Be My Neighbour?

2018 · Documentary

Domain: Radical gentleness with children You will understand: What it means to truly take a child seriously, and how rare that is The benchmark. A man who believed in the inner life of every child — and acted on it. 2. Être et Avoir

2002 · Documentary, France

Domain: A village classroom You will understand: How a great teacher holds space — patience as a form of love One teacher, one class, one year — the most peaceful documentary ever made. 3. Treacle Jr.

2010 · Fiction

Domain: Innocence surviving into adulthood You will understand: What happens when someone never loses their childlike openness A strange, tender British film about a man who simply trusts everyone.

4. Boyhood

2014 · Fiction

Domain: How children actually grow up You will understand: The texture of growing up — boredom, confusion, love, change — from the inside Filmed over 12 years — Linklater lets time itself become the subject.

5. My Neighbor Totoro

1988 · Animation, Japan

Domain: How children perceive the world You will understand: A child's perception is not smaller than an adult's — it is differently complete Miyazaki — no villain, no threat — just a child's way of being in the world. 6. Beasts of the Southern Wild

2012 · Fiction

Domain: Child's eye on poverty and wonder You will understand: How imagination is not escapism but survival — a child's fierce inner world Six-year-old Hushpuppy in the Louisiana bayou — mythic and real.

7. Capernaum

Domain: Childhood abandoned by adults You will understand: What children suffer when the systems meant to protect them simply aren't there A 12-year-old sues his parents for giving him life. Wrenching and necessary. —2—
