Directors · Cities · Eras · Controversies · Human Depths This list makes no apologies. It includes films that are beautiful, brutal, explicit, politically radical, spiritually demanding, and formally extreme. Cinema at its most serious is cinema that refuses to look away. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 1

PAGE 2

Why This List

Cinema's greatest achievements share one quality: they are unwilling to make you comfortable. The films in this list — drawn from every inhabited continent, seven decades of sound cinema, and every genre from the Western to the avant-garde essay film — were chosen because they insist on being taken seriously. This list does not segregate adult filmmaking into a shameful corner. Nagisa Oshima's Ai No Corrida, Bertolucci's Last Tango in Paris, von Trier's Nymphomaniac, Catherine Breillat's Fat Girl, and Gaspar Noé's Love are here because sexuality is one of the primary languages of human experience — and the filmmakers who treat it with the same gravity as mortality, politics, and faith deserve the same critical attention. Explicit cinema is not a genre; it is a choice about what the camera is permitted to witness. The 100 films span 30 genres, 28 countries, and nearly a century of image-making. They include silent masterpieces and digital experiments; Hollywood genre films at their most formally audacious and austere European essays running to nine hours. They are ordered alphabetically by title to resist any implication of ranking — Kubrick and Kalatozov, Bresson and Bong, Varda and Vertov stand as equals. Watch them in whatever order you choose. Watch them more than once. Some of them will require it. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 2

WAR / PSYCHOLOGICAL

01. Apocalypse Now

Francis Ford Coppola · USA · Saigon / Philippines · 1979 A U.S. Army captain journeys upriver into Cambodia to assassinate a rogue colonel who has set himself up as a god. Based on Conrad's Heart of Darkness, the film dissolves the line between civilisation and savagery.

WHY IT MATTERS:

One of cinema's great documents of moral disintegration — every frame a descent into the primal.

ADULT / EROTIC

02. Ai No Corrida (In the Realm of the Senses) Nagisa Oshima · Japan / France · Tokyo · 1976 Based on a true 1936 incident, a former geisha and her employer enter a spiral of obsessive erotic entanglement that ends in annihilation. Unsimulated intimacy is deployed as political provocation against Japan's militarist state.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The most serious film ever made with explicit sex — a meditation on desire as the one radical freedom left.

BIOGRAPHICAL / PHILOSOPHICAL

03. Andrei Rublev

Andrei Tarkovsky · USSR · Medieval Russia · 1966 Episodes from the life of the 15th-century icon painter unfold across an epic, near-silent canvas of faith, cruelty, and artistic vocation in medieval Russia.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A cathedral of a film — slow, vast, and spiritually devastating.

CRIME / DRAMA

04. Amores Perros

Alejandro González Iñárritu · Mexico · Mexico City · 2000 Three interconnected stories — a young man escaping poverty through dogfighting, a supermodel disfigured in a crash, and a hitman — collide in Mexico City's brutal social mosaic.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Raw, labyrinthine, and impossibly alive — the city itself bleeds. —B— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 3

SOCIAL REALISM / DRAMA

05. Bicycle Thieves

Vittorio De Sica · Italy · Rome · 1948

A desperate father and his young son spend a day scouring post-war Rome for the stolen bicycle without which he cannot work. A masterpiece of neo-realist restraint.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Every close-up of the boy's face rewrites what cinema is for.

MYSTERY / PHILOSOPHICAL

06. Blow-Up

Michelangelo Antonioni · Italy / UK · London · 1966 A fashion photographer in swinging London discovers he may have inadvertently photographed a murder — or perhaps nothing at all. Antonioni turns the act of seeing into pure epistemological vertigo.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A film about the impossibility of knowing anything — including what you've just watched.

LGBTQ+ / ADULT / EROTIC

07. Blue Is the Warmest Colour Abdellatif Kechiche · France · Lille / Paris · 2013 A teenage girl's awakening into selfhood, desire, and heartbreak through her consuming love affair with an older art student. Winner of the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Three hours of unsimulated emotional and physical intimacy — a portrait of love as a complete education.

ROMANCE / LGBTQ+

08. Brokeback Mountain

Ang Lee · USA · Wyoming / Texas · 2005 Two ranch hands begin a decades-long secret love affair in the Wyoming mountains, unable to reconcile their love with the world they inhabit.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Ang Lee makes landscape into longing — the mountains stand for every emotion these men cannot speak. —C— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 4

DRAMA / FILM NOIR

09. Citizen Kane

Orson Welles · USA · New York / Xanadu · 1941 A reporter investigates the life of a dead media titan through the recollections of those who knew him, finding a portrait of ambition, loneliness, and the illusion of power.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Still the most technically audacious debut in film history — and the saddest story about success ever told.

THRILLER / PSYCHOLOGICAL

10. Cache (Hidden)

Michael Haneke · France / Austria · Paris · 2005 A prosperous Parisian TV intellectual receives surveillance tapes of his own home, forcing a reckoning with a childhood act of colonial cruelty he has spent decades suppressing.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Haneke weaponises the frame — the screen becomes complicit in the guilt it depicts.

WAR / HORROR

11. Come and See Elem Klimov · USSR · Belarus · 1985 A Belarusian boy joins the partisans and witnesses the systematic Nazi extermination of his village — a film so harrowing it seems to physically age the viewer.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The anti-war film that makes all others look soft. Watching it changes your face. —D—

DRAMA / POLITICAL

12. Dogville

Lars von Trier · Denmark · Rocky Mountains (stage) · 2003 A fugitive woman seeks refuge in a bare-stage Colorado town. The townspeople exact an escalating price for their hospitality — a parable of exploitation, charity, and power stripped to its skeleton.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Von Trier removes every visual comfort so the moral horror is inescapable. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 5

MUSICAL / TRAGEDY

13. Dancer in the Dark Lars von Trier · Denmark · Small-town USA · 2000 A Czech immigrant in 1960s America, going blind, escapes into movie musicals in her mind while reality closes in with terrible finality.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Björk's performance and von Trier's Dogme aesthetic collide to create a grief machine.

PSYCHOLOGICAL / SURREALIST

14. Dogtooth (Kynodontas)

Yorgos Lanthimos · Greece · Athens (suburban compound) · 2009 Three adult children raised in total isolation by their parents, taught a distorted language and fictional rules of the world — until one fissure cracks everything.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A diagram of how ideology is installed in a person — family as totalitarian state. —E—

ADULT / EROTIC / MYSTERY

15. Eyes Wide Shut

Stanley Kubrick · USA / UK · New York · 1999 A Manhattan doctor's one night of sexual wandering — triggered by his wife's erotic confession — leads him into a secret society ritual. Kubrick's final film is about the erotic life hidden beneath bourgeois respectability.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Kubrick uses sex as a mirror of class and power — the orgy scenes are simultaneously opulent and terrifying.

SCI-FI / ROMANCE

16. Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind Michel Gondry · USA · New York / Montauk · 2004 A man undergoes a procedure to erase his ex-girlfriend from his memory — only to fall in love with her again inside the deletion. Kaufman's script is a map of what we can't help remembering.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Love reimagined as architecture of the mind — melancholy and exhilarating in equal measure. —F— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 6

DRAMA / FANTASY

17. Fanny and Alexander

Ingmar Bergman · Sweden · Uppsala · 1982

Bergman's farewell epic — a bourgeois family in early 20th-century Sweden seen through a child's eyes in which theatre, magic, death, and tyranny are equally real.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The warmest film by cinema's great cold master — a summation of a life's work in light.

PSYCHOLOGICAL / BLACK COMEDY

18. Fight Club

David Fincher · USA · Unnamed American city · 1999 An insomniac office worker and a soap salesman build an underground fight network that metastasises into anarchist terror. A diagnosis of late-capitalist masculine rage.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The film seduces you into the ideology it critiques — that is its genius.

THRILLER / HORROR

19. Funny Games

Michael Haneke · Austria · Austrian lake district · 1997 Two polite young men hold a bourgeois family hostage and systematically torture them — periodically breaking the fourth wall to implicate the audience in their own desire for screen violence.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A film that hates you for watching it, and is right. —G—

ANIMATION / WAR

20. Grave of the Fireflies Isao Takahata · Japan · Kobe / Osaka · 1988 Two orphaned siblings struggle to survive in Japan during the final months of WWII — an animated film of such emotional honesty it dismantles the very idea of animation as child-friendly.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Roger Ebert called it one of the greatest war films ever made. He was correct. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 7

DRAMA / EXISTENTIAL

21. Goodbye Dragon Inn

Tsai Ming-liang · Taiwan · Taipei · 2003 The last night of a crumbling Taipei cinema, nearly empty, screening King Hu's 1966 epic. Tsai makes the ghosts of film history sit in the audience with us.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The most beautiful elegy to cinema ever made — almost entirely silent. —T—

WAR / DRAMA

22. The Human Condition (trilogy)

Masaki Kobayashi · Japan · Manchuria · 1959–61

Nearly ten hours following a Japanese pacifist through the horrors of WWII-era Manchuria — forced labour, front-line combat, and Soviet captivity. The definitive humanist anti-war statement.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A monument of moral seriousness — cinema at the scale of the novel. —H—

NEW WAVE / ROMANCE

23. Hiroshima Mon Amour

Alain Resnais · France / Japan · Hiroshima · 1959 A French actress and a Japanese architect conduct a 24-hour love affair in post-atomic Hiroshima, their bodies carrying the trauma of the war. Marguerite Duras wrote the script.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Memory and forgetting as political and erotic acts — the founding text of the French New Wave. —I— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 8

DRAMA / PHILOSOPHICAL

24. Ikiru (To Live)

Akira Kurosawa · Japan · Tokyo · 1952

A Tokyo bureaucrat, diagnosed with terminal cancer, attempts to find meaning in the last months of his life. Kurosawa's most intimate and devastating film.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The question 'what does it mean to live?' has never been asked more gently or more urgently.

ROMANCE / DRAMA

25. In the Mood for Love Wong Kar-wai · Hong Kong · Hong Kong (1962) · 2000 Two neighbours in 1960s Hong Kong discover their spouses are having an affair and fall into a yearning that they are too honourable to act upon. The entire film is the space between them.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Desire as pure cinema — every slow-motion staircase a universe. —J—

NEW WAVE / ROMANCE

26. Jules and Jim François Truffaut · France · Paris / Germany · 1962 Two friends — French and Austrian — share a mercurial, free-spirited woman across decades and two world wars. The joy and the tragedy are inseparable.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Truffaut makes freedom look both glorious and fatal at the same time.

DRAMA / FEMINIST

27. Jeanne Dielman, 23 Quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles

Chantal Akerman · Belgium · Brussels · 1975

Over three and a half hours we watch a widowed mother perform domestic tasks with clockwork precision — until the routine cracks. The film uses boredom as a political weapon.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Named the greatest film of all time by Sight & Sound 2022 — it earns that with complete deliberateness. —K— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 9

SOCIAL REALISM / DRAMA

28. Kes

Ken Loach · UK · Barnsley, Yorkshire · 1969 A neglected mining-town boy trains a kestrel — the one sovereign thing in his life — while the world systematically denies him any future. Devastating working-class poetry.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A film that genuinely loves its subject — which makes what happens unbearable.

PSYCHOLOGICAL / HORROR

29. Killing of a Sacred Deer Yorgos Lanthimos · Greece / UK · Cincinnati · 2017 A cardiac surgeon's family begins to inexplicably deteriorate after he befriends a teenage boy connected to a past medical failure. Greek tragedy reborn in American suburbia.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Lanthimos uses flat affect and Euripidean logic to make dread entirely formal. —L—

ADULT / EROTIC / DRAMA

30. Last Tango in Paris Bernardo Bertolucci · France / Italy · Paris · 1972 A grieving American and a young Parisian woman conduct an anonymous, rule-bound erotic relationship in an empty apartment — no names, no past, only the body as negotiation of grief.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Brando's improvised monologues on loss remain some of the most courageous acting ever filmed.

SOCIAL REALISM / SURREALIST

31. Los Olvidados

Luis Buñuel · Mexico · Mexico City · 1950

Street children in Mexico City's slums destroy each other through cycles of poverty, violence, and institutional abandonment. Buñuel shoots documentary realism and nightmare in the same frame.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The original film about systemic child poverty — still unsurpassed in its angry clarity. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 10

MYSTERY / DRAMA

32. L'Avventura

Michelangelo Antonioni · Italy · Sicily / Rome · 1960 A woman disappears during a cruise among islands and is simply never found. Her lover and best friend drift into each other's arms. Antonioni makes absence the protagonist.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The mystery is never solved because the point is that we move on — the most honest film about grief and infidelity. —M—

CRIME / THRILLER

33. M

Fritz Lang · Germany · Berlin · 1931

A child murderer terrorising Berlin is hunted simultaneously by police and the criminal underworld. Lang invents the serial-killer procedural and asks who has the right to judge.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Peter Lorre's performance in the kangaroo-court finale is the most disturbing thing in pre-war cinema.

SURREALIST / MYSTERY

34. Mulholland Drive

David Lynch · USA · Los Angeles · 2001 An amnesiac woman and a naïve actress spiral through dreamlike Los Angeles in a film that devours itself at the two-hour mark and reveals the dream to be a dying woman's wish-fulfillment.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The definitive film about Hollywood — and the definitive film about how the mind lies to itself.

DRAMA / LGBTQ+

35. Moonlight

Barry Jenkins · USA · Miami (Liberty City) · 2016 A young Black man's life in three chapters — boyhood, adolescence, adulthood — shaped by poverty, a crack-addicted mother, and a buried love. Jenkins films tenderness as if it were endangered.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The most lyrical depiction of Black queer interiority in American cinema. —N— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 11

ADULT / EROTIC / DRAMA

36. Nymphomaniac (Vol. I & II) Lars von Trier · Denmark · Copenhagen / various · 2013 A self-declared nymphomaniac recounts her erotic life to the man who found her beaten in an alley — a four-hour Dickensian confession that treats sexuality with the same moral weight as religion.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Von Trier's most ambitious film — a philosophical novel in which sex is the language of self-knowledge.

MUSICAL / POLITICAL

37. Nashville

Robert Altman · USA · Nashville, Tennessee · 1975 Twenty-four characters orbit a political rally and a country-music festival in America's bicentennial year. Altman creates a portrait of a nation in which celebrity and democracy have fused.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The great American film — vast, funny, sad, and prophetic. —O—

THRILLER / MYSTERY

38. Oldboy

Park Chan-wook · South Korea · Seoul · 2003 A man imprisoned for fifteen years without explanation is released and given five days to discover why. The answer destroys every assumption the film has built — Sophocles in a Seoul alleyway.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The twist doesn't just shock — it retroactively poisons every frame that precedes it.

DRAMA / PHILOSOPHICAL

39. Ordet (The Word)

Carl Theodor Dreyer · Denmark · Jutland · 1955

On a Jutland farm, religious faith manifests as literal resurrection. Dreyer films a miracle with the same camera distance and patience he gives to daily chores.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The final scene is the most quietly radical act in the history of cinema. —P— 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 12

BLACK COMEDY / THRILLER

40. Parasite

Bong Joon-ho · South Korea · Seoul · 2019 A poor family insinuate themselves into a wealthy household as tutors and drivers — until the basement reveals what the class war actually looks like underground.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Bong Joon-ho makes a genre film that is also a Marxist diagram — and you can't see the join.

PSYCHOLOGICAL / AVANT-GARDE

41. Persona

Ingmar Bergman · Sweden · Remote island · 1966 A famous actress suddenly stops speaking. Her nurse accompanies her to a seaside cottage. Their identities begin to dissolve into each other. Bergman makes cinema interrogate itself.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The film asks what it means to be a self — and doesn't care if that question destroys the film to answer it.

HORROR / THRILLER

42. Psycho

Alfred Hitchcock · USA · Phoenix / Fairvale, California · 1960 A secretary on the run with stolen money stops at a roadside motel run by a nervous young man and his domineering mother. Hitchcock kills his star in reel one and rebuilds cinema's contract with the audience.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Still the most formally aggressive film ever made by a major Hollywood studio. —R—

MYSTERY / DRAMA

43. Rashomon

Akira Kurosawa · Japan · Feudal Kyoto · 1950

Four witnesses give irreconcilable accounts of a samurai's murder and his wife's rape. Kurosawa gave cinema a word — the Rashomon effect — for the impossibility of objective truth.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A film built entirely from the architecture of unreliable narration. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 13

WAR / SOCIAL REALISM

44. Rome, Open City

Roberto Rossellini · Italy · Rome · 1945

Shot in secret on Rome's streets during the final Nazi occupation, this document of resistance and betrayal launched Italian neo-realism. Real bombed streets, non-professional actors, raw grief.

WHY IT MATTERS:

A film made under occupation about resistance — the most important birth announcement in film history.

WAR / DRAMA

45. Ran

Akira Kurosawa · Japan · Feudal Japan · 1985

Kurosawa's King Lear — an ageing warlord divides his kingdom among three sons with catastrophic results. The battle sequences remain the most visually stunning in world cinema.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Kurosawa at 75, painting with armies. The scale of grief matches the scale of the compositions. —S—

SCI-FI / PHILOSOPHICAL

46. Stalker

Andrei Tarkovsky · USSR · Industrial wasteland / The Zone · 1979 A guide leads a writer and a professor into a forbidden post-apocalyptic zone where a room is said to grant one's deepest wish. Almost nothing happens. Everything happens.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Cinema as religious experience — Tarkovsky requires surrender.

FILM NOIR / DRAMA

47. Sunset Boulevard

Billy Wilder · USA · Hollywood, Los Angeles · 1950 A fading silent-film star and a cynical screenwriter trap each other in mutual delusion in her crumbling mansion. Hollywood eating itself, narrated by a corpse.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The most savage film ever made about the industry that made it. 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen · Page 14

DOCUMENTARY

48. Shoah

Claude Lanzmann · France · Poland / multiple sites · 1985 Nine and a half hours. No archival footage — only testimony. Survivors, bystanders, and perpetrators of the Holocaust speak at the sites where it happened. An act of cinema as an act of witness.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The ethical extreme of documentary filmmaking — what testimony costs both speaker and listener.

DRAMA / FANTASY

49. The Spirit of the Beehive Víctor Erice · Spain · Castilian village, 1940 · 1973 A six-year-old girl in Franco's Spain becomes obsessed with Frankenstein after a cinema screening, entering a private mythology that lets her survive historical and domestic silence.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The greatest film about childhood — and about what art does to a mind that needs it to survive.

DRAMA / EXISTENTIAL

50. Sátántangó

Béla Tarr · Hungary · Collapsing Hungarian collective · 1994 Over seven and a half hours, a community on a decaying collective farm awaits the return of a charismatic swindler in a loop of mud, rain, and false hope. Shot in long, unbroken takes.

WHY IT MATTERS:

Watching it is a different relationship to time — cinema as duration itself.

DRAMA / FAMILY

51. Tokyo Story

Yasujiro Ozu · Japan · Tokyo / Onomichi · 1953 An elderly couple visit their grown children in Tokyo and find only indifference. Their daughter-in-law, a widow, is the only one who shows them kindness. Ozu's most heartbreaking film.

WHY IT MATTERS:

The most perfectly constructed sad film in existence — every cut is a small goodbye.
