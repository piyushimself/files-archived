HOW TO USE THIS CURRICULUM

The Cycle

This curriculum circles death seven times. Each circle is closer than the last. Between each circle is a retreat — books and films that pull you back into the aliveness that death makes possible. Do not skip the retreats. They are not rest stops — they are the reason the

circles exist.

The Pace

One entry per week is the ideal pace. That is two years. Some weeks you will need more time with an entry. Take it. Some entries you

will need to sit with for days before the meditation prompt can be answered. This is correct.

The Journal

Keep a journal alongside this curriculum. After each entry, write one thing it changed or questioned in you. At the end of two years, this journal will be the most important document about your inner life that you have ever produced.

The Repeats

Several entries appear more than once — watched or read again at a deeper point in the curriculum. This is deliberate. The same work means completely different things to a different self. You will not be the same person who encountered it first.

The Meditation Prompts

Each entry has a meditation prompt in the final column. These are not rhetorical. They require actual response — writing, sitting,

going outside, calling someone. The curriculum is not complete if the prompts are ignored.

The Difficulty

Some entries are very difficult — Come and See, Salo, the death of Ivan Ilyich. You are not required to complete them. But know that the curriculum places them where it does for a reason. Each difficult entry comes after you have been prepared by what precedes it.

The End

The curriculum ends with Ikiru — a man on a swing in the snow, humming. He built the park. He is dying. He is content. That is the curriculum's entire argument, distilled to one image. When you arrive there the second time, you will understand it from inside.

WHAT THIS CURRICULUM DOES

After Circle One

Death becomes a subject you can think about without flinching. You have a philosophical distance that is honest rather than

defensive.

After Circle Two

Death is in your body. You feel your own mortality in a new way — not as horror but as fact. The body's relationship with its ending

has become conscious.

After Circle Three

Death has become your teacher. You use it. You hold it close when you need to know what matters. The samurai's clarity is available

to you.

After Circle Four

You have sat with every tradition's answer and found each one partially true. You are building your own relationship with death — not

inherited, but chosen.

After Circle Five

You have grieved — perhaps for the first time fully. The grief has moved through you rather than being held frozen. You are lighter for

it, not heavier.

After Circle Six

You have sat inside death without armour. You are not the same person who entered this curriculum. The fear has not disappeared —

it has become familiar.

After Circle Seven

Death is befriended. Not conquered. Not transcended. Befriended. It sits with you quietly, and you are grateful for its company —

because it is the thing that makes every day matter.

After All Seven

You will live differently. Not dramatically — quietly. You will notice more. You will postpone less. You will love with more specificity and urgency. You will be less afraid of the things that do not matter because you have sat with the thing that does. The curriculum ends where it began — with a man on a swing in the snow. Humming.
