200 Books Every Filmmaker Should Know

The books that directors, cinematographers, writers and editors have carried with them — cited in interviews,

dedications, masterclasses. Akira Kurosawa kept Dostoevsky at his bedside. Andrei Tarkovsky wrote a treatise on time. Jean-Luc

Godard made films out of Faulkner and Céline. Stanley Kubrick annotated his scripts with philosophy. Every great filmmaker is a great reader first. This list is organized not by difficulty but by what it gives you: the craft of storytelling, the grammar of the image, the architecture of narrative, the psychology of character, the history of the form, and the deeper human questions that every great film must answer. Each entry names the filmmakers who

have cited it — so you can trace the reading life behind the work you love. 15 Categories · 200 Books · A Cinema Education

Craft

Cited by: Cited by Kubrick, Paddy Chayefsky, Horton Foote Premise, character, conflict — the Hungarian dramatist whose three pillars hold every story up.

A Filmmaker's Reading List 200 Essential Books

What These Books Give You

Technique without vision is craft. Vision without technique is intention. These 200 books address

both — and they address the deeper question underneath both: what is a film for? The filmmakers cited throughout this list did not read these books to extract usable information.

They read them to become different people — people with larger inner worlds, richer emotional

memories, more complex understanding of what human beings are. That is what a great film requires. Not a knowledge of three-act structure. A knowledge of being human. Bresson's Notes on the Cinematograph has 121 aphorisms. You can read it in two hours. It will take ten years to understand. Tarkovsky's Sculpting in Time is 240 pages. Read it before your first

film. Read it again after your tenth. It will be a different book each time. "I read. I read enormously. Books are more important to me than films."

— Ingmar Bergman

"To be a filmmaker you must be a poet. And to be a poet you must read."

— Akira Kurosawa

"A story should have a beginning, a middle and an end, but not necessarily in that order."

— Jean-Luc Godard

"Cinema uses the image to capture time. But it is in literature that time first learned to speak."

— Andrei Tarkovsky

The camera is the last thing you pick up. — 24 —
