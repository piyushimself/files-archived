200 Writing Prompts

for Emotion & Wonder A sequential course in human experience — seven phases of writing practice moving from the interior world through intimacy, suffering, wonder, time, identity, and finally to the largest questions

of meaning and existence. Work through the phases in order. Each builds on what came before.

Each prompt includes guidance on how to approach it. Each phase includes a film sequence to

watch alongside the writing.

PROMPTS 1–25 · THE INTERIOR WORLD

THE SELF IN STILLNESS

Before you can write about others, you must learn to sit with yourself. These prompts ask you to

slow down, notice, and feel without judgment. No plot needed — only presence.

FILM SEQUENCE FOR THIS PHASE

1 Paterson

Jim Jarmusch, 2016

Teaches you to find the extraordinary in routine. A bus driver writes poems. Nothing happens, and

everything happens. Watch before prompts 1–5. 2 The Tree of Life

Terrence Malick, 2011

An essay in pure sensation and memory — images that bypass language and land directly in the

body. Watch before prompts 10–15.

3 Yi Yi

Edward Yang, 2000

Slow, tender attention to ordinary life across three generations. The most patient film ever made

about the interior life of a family. Watch before prompts 20–25.

PROMPTS 26–50 · INTIMACY & CONNECTION

THE TENDERNESS OF OTHERS

Now you turn outward. These prompts explore the terrifying and beautiful project of truly seeing

another person — and allowing yourself to be seen. They require vulnerability, precision, and

compassion.

FILM SEQUENCE FOR THIS PHASE

1 Moonlight

Barry Jenkins, 2016

Three chapters of a life — the grammar of tenderness between people who cannot speak it. Every

frame is about what remains when words fail. Watch before prompts 26–30.

2 Marriage Story

Noah Baumbach, 2019

Two people who love each other and cannot save each other. A film about the labour of connection

and its cost. Watch before prompts 38–42.

3 Aftersun

Charlotte Wells, 2022

A masterclass in what is unsaid between people who love each other. What we miss in the people

closest to us. Watch before prompts 46–50.

PROMPTS 51–80 · SUFFERING & SURVIVAL

FEAR, GRIEF & THE DARK ROOMS

These are the hardest prompts. Do not rush them. The goal is not to wallow but to transform — to find language for the parts of human experience that resist language. Write slowly. Take breaks.

This is where real writing begins.

  • If these prompts surface something acute, pause. Share the work with someone you trust before

continuing. These prompts are not therapy — but they may open territory that needs careful tending.

FILM SEQUENCE FOR THIS PHASE

1 Manchester by the Sea

Kenneth Lonergan, 2016

Grief that cannot be resolved, and the bravery of portraying that honestly. The film refuses

catharsis — which is its great gift. Watch before prompts 51–60. 2 Grave of the Fireflies

Isao Takahata, 1988

Loss rendered with absolute clarity. Possibly the most devastating film ever made. Watch alone.

Watch before prompts 65–70.

3 First Reformed

Paul Schrader, 2017

Spiritual despair and the question of meaning in the face of the world's condition. A diary film.

Watch before prompts 75–80.
