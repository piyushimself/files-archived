Mulholland Drive

David Lynch · USA · 2001 IMDb ★★★★■ 7.9/10 RT 83% ■ ■ Mubi An aspiring actress arrives in Los Angeles and meets an amnesiac woman after a car crash on Mulholland Drive. Lynch builds a labyrinthine dream logic — Hollywood as a machine that devours its dreamers —

before a devastating structural inversion reveals the truth. WHY IT MATTERS The definitive film about Hollywood's darkness — and one of cinema's most complete

expressions of dream logic as narrative structure. #02 FILM PSYCHOLOGICAL CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

Persona

Ingmar Bergman · Sweden · 1966

IMDb ★★★★■ 8.1/10 RT 91% ■ ■ Criterion Channel, Mubi A nurse is assigned to care for an actress who has inexplicably stopped speaking. In isolation, their identities begin to dissolve into each other. Bergman's most formally radical film — the two women become aspects of

one psyche. WHY IT MATTERS The most complete philosophical film about identity ever made — and the one that most openly

shows cinema examining itself. #03 FILM PSYCHOLOGICAL CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

Repulsion

Roman Polanski · UK · 1965 IMDb ★★★★■ 7.9/10 RT 96% ■ ■ Criterion Channel A Belgian manicurist left alone in her London flat descends into violent psychosis as her sexual repulsion and fear of men takes hold. Shot in claustrophobic black and white, the apartment itself becomes her

disintegrating mind. WHY IT MATTERS The first great film of female psychological horror — told entirely from inside a breakdown,

without judgment or explanation. —3—

Dogtooth

Yorgos Lanthimos · Greece · 2009

Three adult children raised in total isolation by their parents, taught a distorted language and a fictional world — until one fissure cracks everything open. Lanthimos makes ideology visible by rendering it absurd. WHY IT MATTERS A diagram of how families — and states — manufacture reality. The most precise film about

control ever made. #05 FILM PSYCHOLOGICAL CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

Cache (Hidden)

Michael Haneke · France / Austria · 2005

A prosperous Parisian TV intellectual receives anonymous surveillance tapes of his home, forcing a reckoning with a childhood act of colonial cruelty he has suppressed for decades. Haneke's camera indicts

the viewer as complicit. WHY IT MATTERS The most intelligent film about white liberal guilt — and about how surveillance has always been

a tool of power over the powerless. #06 FILM PSYCHOLOGICAL CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

Eyes Wide Shut

Stanley Kubrick · USA / UK · 1999 IMDb ★★★★■ 7.5/10 RT 76% ■ ■ Max A Manhattan doctor's one night of sexual wandering — triggered by his wife's erotic confession — leads him into a secret society ritual. Kubrick's final film is about the erotic life hidden beneath bourgeois respectability. WHY IT MATTERS Kubrick uses sex as a mirror of class and power — the orgy scenes are simultaneously opulent

and terrifying. #07 FILM PSYCHOLOGICAL CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

Black Swan

Darren Aronofsky · USA · 2010 IMDb ★★★★■ 8.0/10 RT 87% ■ ■ Disney+ A ballet dancer pushed to her limits by a demanding director and a seductive rival loses her grip on reality as she attempts to embody both the White and Black Swan in Swan Lake. Body horror as artistic perfectionism. WHY IT MATTERS The most visceral portrait of the price of perfection — Portman's performance and Aronofsky's

kineticism are inseparable from each other. —4—

Sátántangó

Béla Tarr · Hungary · 1994

Over seven and a half hours, a dying collective farm awaits a charismatic figure who may or may not bring salvation. Shot in long, hypnotic black-and-white takes of rain, mud, and waiting. A meditation on hope and

manipulation. WHY IT MATTERS The only film that earns its seven hours — watching it is an experience that permanently

changes your relationship to cinematic time. #18 FILM WORLD CINEMA 100 Films That Demand to Be Seen

silences, music that cuts in like memory. WHY IT MATTERS The greatest film about grief's desire to disappear — and why the self refuses to let you.
